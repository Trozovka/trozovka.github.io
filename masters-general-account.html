The Master's General Account in One Workbook

How I turned a pile of linked spreadsheets into one checked Excel file

By John Winsome Munar (Trozovka), Master Mariner · November 2026 · Also on GitHub

Every month the Master sends the office a general account: crew wages, allotments to families, cash advances, bonded store sales, allowances paid in cash, and the cash left in the safe. Most companies give you an Excel form for it. Most Masters then build their own helper files around that form, because the form alone does not help you get the numbers right.

I did the same for years. My helper files grew until they caused more mistakes than they prevented. This page explains what was wrong, what I changed, and the rules I now follow, so another Master (or a developer helping one) can do the same with their own company's form.

Nothing here is copied from any company form. All numbers are made up.

2. Where my old setup went wrong

  • Links to other files. My helper files pulled numbers from the company file by external links. Every month the file got a new name or moved to a new folder, and the links either broke or, worse, kept reading last month's file without any warning. One of my files was still linked to a copy on a USB stick.
  • The same crew list typed six times. Cash advances, cash received, bonuses, allowances, bonded store and the company form each had their own list of names. One spelling change and the totals stopped matching.
  • Rates typed inside formulas. Each wage cell held the monthly rate inside the formula itself. When the company changed the scale, every row had to be edited by hand.
  • The allotment was a typed number. I worked it out on the side so that nothing was carried forward, then typed it in. Any later change to a deduction meant doing it again.
  • Checks that depended on the links. My cross-check file was only right when its links happened to point at the right file.
  • Rounding everywhere and nowhere. Some sheets rounded to cents, others did not, so totals that should agree were a few cents apart and I never knew which one was right.

3. The rules I set myself

  1. The company form is never changed. It is their document. My file only prepares numbers for it.
  2. One file per month. No links to any other file.
  3. Type each fact once. A crew member's name, rank and dates are typed in one place; every other sheet reads them from there.
  4. Plain formulas only. No macros (company laptops often block them) and only functions that work in old Excel versions, so any Master can open it.
  5. Same cell addresses as the company sheet wherever numbers are copied across, so a block can be pasted straight in.
  6. Checks on screen. Green when right, red when wrong, amber when it might be intended.
  7. One rounding rule. Money that leaves the ship (allotments) is rounded to cents; the tiny remainder is carried forward and stays visible.

5. Wages: pro-rata by 30 days

Many companies pay a full month as 30 days, whatever the calendar says, and a part month by the actual days on board. With a made-up basic wage of 900 a month:

  • On board the whole month: 900 × 30 / 30 = 900
  • Joined on the 6th of a 31-day month (26 days): 900 × 26 / 30 = 780

The days fill in by themselves from the sign-on and sign-off dates:

=IF(name="","",
  IF(AND(OR(signon="",signon<=month_start), OR(signoff="",signoff>=EOMONTH(month_start,0))),
     30,
     MIN(IF(signoff="",EOMONTH(month_start,0),signoff),EOMONTH(month_start,0))
     - MAX(IF(signon="",month_start,signon),month_start) + 1))

Each wage part is then a lookup into the RATES sheet by rank, times days / 30. One part (a personal allowance) differs from person to person even within the same rank, so it sits on the CREW sheet instead.

6. Let the allotment calculate itself

The regular allotment is simply what is left:

allotment = earned wage + other incomes + last month's balance
          - all deductions - anything the crew member wants kept on board

rounded to cents. If a crew member ends up owing money (for example a large cash advance on a short month), the allotment goes negative. A negative allotment cannot be sent, so the CHECKS sheet shows it in amber: it might be intended, but you should look.

Many crew send money to more than one person: a fixed amount to one, the rest to another. Each beneficiary is one line. Type the fixed amount on the first line, leave the second blank, and the blank line takes whatever is left:

=IF(fixed<>"", fixed,
   IF(first line of this person without a fixed amount,
      ROUND(person's allotment - SUMIFS(fixed amounts of this person), 2), 0))

The lines of all crew must add up to the payroll total. That is one of the checks.

7. Bonded store and the handler's commission

Bonded items are bought from a different supplier in each port, so the price is typed per article, once. Quantities are typed per person. Each person's total is SUMPRODUCT(quantities, prices), which goes straight into their deductions. No long hand-made qty×price + qty×price + ... formulas that silently miss a column.

If the selling price is cost plus a markup that pays the store handler, the commission can be worked out from total sales. With a 5% markup:

commission = total sales / 1.05 × 0.05

Stock carried forward is stock last month + purchased − sold − used for entertainment. A negative number means a typing error, and it turns red.

8. Getting the numbers into the company form

The TRANSFER sheet uses the same cell addresses as the company sheet. You select a white block, copy, click the same cell in the company file, and use Paste Special → Values. Columns that are formulas in the company form are shaded grey, and you never paste over them.

This is why "same cell addresses" is one of the rules: there is nothing to map by hand, so nothing to get wrong.

9. Checks you can see

Check Why
Every rank exists in the rate table A typo in a rank would quietly pay zero
Days between 1 and 30 Catches wrong dates
Every income line belongs to a crew member Catches name spelling differences
Every income line has a type So the payslip can be explained later
Negative allotment (amber) Crew owing money
Carried forward = what you wanted The allotment really took everything else
Income total and cash advance total agree across sheets Nothing lost between sheets
Allotment lines = payroll allotments Every dollar has a beneficiary
Allotment > 0 but no beneficiary line Someone would not get paid
Negative bonded stock Typing error
Cash counted = cash balance The safe matches the books
Company totals typed back in After pasting, the company form agrees with this file

The last check replaces the external links I used before. After pasting into the company form, you type its handful of totals back into the CHECKS sheet. Six numbers typed by hand are more reliable than six links that may point at the wrong file.

10. Testing it with a real past month

The only test I trust: take a month that was already sent and accepted, put its data into the new file, and require every line to match the old result to the cent. Wages, incomes, allotments, bonded, cash advances, the commission and the final cash balance.

It matched on every line. Along the way the checks also showed two things the old files had hidden: a crew member who left owing money (I had planted that one on purpose, to see if it would be caught), and two parts of the company form that disagreed with each other about that same person. Both would have been caught before sending.

11. The Excel functions I used

All available since Excel 2010, so they work on old office laptops:

Function Used for
INDEX + MATCH Looking up a rank's rates, a person's allotment
SUMIFS, COUNTIFS Totals per person, "first line of this person"
SUMPRODUCT Quantity × price across a row, counting problems in a range
EOMONTH Month end for the day count
ROUND One rounding rule, in one place
Data validation lists Rank and name drop-downs, so spelling always matches
Conditional formatting Green / red / amber checks

No XLOOKUP, no FILTER, no macros.

12. Lessons

  • Type each fact once. Most of my old mistakes were the same fact typed twice and drifting apart.
  • Links between files are a trap for anything renamed monthly. One file per month, no links.
  • A typed balancing number is a mistake waiting to happen. Calculate it.
  • Test against a month you already know is right. "It looks right" is not a test.
  • Make checks visible. A check hidden in another file is a check nobody runs.

13. Disclaimer

This is how I organise my own monthly account. It is not any company's official form or procedure. Your company's rules, forms and deadlines always come first; if your company counts days, rounds or deducts differently, change the file to match them.
